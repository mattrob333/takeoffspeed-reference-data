# Best practice: erosion and sediment control, stormwater pond/basin, and landscaping - quantities and material types only

Scope: temporary erosion, sediment and pollution control (ESPC: silt fence, compost filter sock, inlet protection, stone construction exit, concrete washout, retrofits, disturbed-area stabilization by mulch / temporary seed / permanent seed / sod, dust control, slope matting), the stone-and-geotextile infiltration basin, and landscape planting (trees, shrubs, mulch, sod, topsoil, irrigation). Prices, labor and productivity are out of scope.

How to read this file. Every factual claim carries a link. Tags: **(primary)** = government manual, standard or fact sheet that I downloaded and read; **(secondary)** = extension or vendor page, read; **(model)** = a second opinion from GPT-6 Astra, unsourced and not independently verified (its own citations were not checked); **(project)** = a file in this repository (`source/drawings/...`, `reference/estimate/lines.json`, `reference/markups/legends.json`). Arithmetic marked **derived** was done in Python.

Honest summary of the evidence. There is no single published "erosion and landscape takeoff standard". What is citable: the Georgia state manual's practice-by-practice installation rules (the job's drawings paste its pages onto the plan set), the US EPA BMP fact sheets, the North Carolina manual (spacing, laps, anchoring, topsoil), and a university extension page for mulch coverage. What is NOT citable from anything I could read: sod pallet coverage, pine-straw bale coverage, blanket staple counts per square yard, stone tons per CY, geotextile seam allowances and waste percentages. Those are estimator convention or supplier data, and my only source is the model's opinion, so they are tagged (model) and must be confirmed with the supplier. I could not use web search (the session's search budget was exhausted), so sources were found by fetching known agency URLs directly; RSMeans and CSI MasterFormat unit conventions could not be read and are not cited.

## 3. Standard measurement rules

### 3.1 Sediment barriers (silt fence, compost filter sock, wattle)
- Measure LF along the drawn alignment on the **phase where it is first installed**; do not carry the same line again on later phases (project C-2.0 to C-2.2 legends repeat codes; model).
- Fence is not placed across concentrated flow, ditches or streams (GSWCC Sd1 p.106, primary; [EPA silt fences](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-silt-fences.pdf), primary).
- Ends and J-hooks: EPA requires the bottom of each end to be higher than the top of the middle and shows J-hooks that turn up the slope to break long runs ([EPA silt fences](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-silt-fences.pdf), primary). NC wants sock ends pointing upslope, a minimum of 10 LF at each end at about 30 degrees ([NC 6.66](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/erosion-and-sediment-control/esc-design-manual/chapter-66-sediment-traps-and-barriers-jan-2026-revision/open), primary). Add that length only if the drawn line does not already include the upturn (model: "add actual LF for uphill returns/J-hooks to installed length").
- Joints: Georgia says overlap barriers 18 in when different types of barrier meet in one run (GSWCC Sd1 p.107, primary). NC says to build the fence from one continuous roll to avoid joints and, where a joint is unavoidable, to fasten the fabric only at a support post with a minimum overlap to the next post; the overlap number is lost in the NC PDF's broken font encoding, so I did not use it (model gives a 2-3 ft lap, unverified). Compost socks over 200 ft are made in sections that are "sleeved" 1-2 ft into each other ([NC 6.66](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/erosion-and-sediment-control/esc-design-manual/chapter-66-sediment-traps-and-barriers-jan-2026-revision/open), primary). A sleeve is physical length consumed, not extra protected line.
- Post/stake count for one open run: ceil(run / spacing) + 1, per run, not per total LF (derived; same formula in model). Example (derived): 100 LF needs 26 posts at 4 ft, 18 at 6 ft, 14 at 8 ft, 11 at 10 ft. For this job's 767.9 LF of sock at 2 ft o.c. a single run needs 385 stakes, at 4 ft 193, at 10 ft 78 (derived), so the detail spacing, not a default, controls the stake count by a factor of five.
- Reasonableness check: EPA's rule of thumb is at least 100 ft of silt fence per 10,000 SF of disturbed area ([EPA silt fences](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-silt-fences.pdf), primary). This job: 767.9 LF on 0.9 acre = 39,204 SF disturbed gives 196 LF per 10,000 SF, about twice the rule of thumb (derived; project C-2.3 gives 0.9 acre of disturbance).
- Sock check dams: drainage area limits and a stake at the center of the channel and at the bed/bank junction, <= 2 ft apart, sock at least 18 in (GSWCC Cd-Fs p.68, primary). A sock at 12 in diameter is spaced 100 ft apart on a 1 percent channel and 20 ft on a 5 percent channel (NC Table 6.66b, [NC 6.66](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/erosion-and-sediment-control/esc-design-manual/chapter-66-sediment-traps-and-barriers-jan-2026-revision/open), primary).
- Fiber rolls (if a job uses them): 8 in minimum diameter, trench at least 2 in deep, stakes about every 4 ft, ends turned upslope; rows spaced 10 ft on 1:1, 20 ft on 2:1, 30 ft on 3:1, 40 ft on 4:1 or flatter ([EPA fiber rolls](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-fiber-rolls.pdf), primary).

### 3.2 Inlet protection
- Count inlets per phase, by type, from the phase plan legend symbols; protection that is placed once is not re-counted on a later phase unless the legend says it changes type (here phase 2 carries fabric-with-frame Sd2-F and phase 3 curb inlet protection Sd2-P) (project C-2.1, C-2.2).
- LF where the legend measures it: for a fabric-frame the quantity is the perimeter of the frame (2 x (L + W) of the grate/box), posts at <= 3 ft (GSWCC Sd2 p.113, primary). For a curb inlet the protection spans the throat plus returns (project convention; GSWCC Sd2-P shows the wrapped block spanning the catch basin inlet with openings facing outward, p.116).
- Limits that signal an error: EPA says a single inlet control normally serves no more than 1 acre, and most jurisdictions cap fabric height at 1.5 ft ([EPA inlet protection](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-storm-drain-inlet-protection.pdf), primary). NC wire-mesh and stone version: 5-ft steel posts driven 2 ft, spaced <= 4 ft, stone height 16 in on a 2:1 outside slope ([NC 6.51](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/land-quality/erosion-and-sediment-control-planning-and-design-manual/chapter-6/vi-inlet-protection/download), primary).
- Job check (derived): the phase 2 legend gives 193.7 FT for Sd2-F, and the C-2.1 markup draws the Sd2-F frame at 4 inlets (4 green squares, 4 `Sd2-F` tags on the plan; project p66), so 193.7 / 4 = 48.4 LF per inlet, i.e. a frame about 12 ft square around each inlet. A frame around a 2 x 3 ft grate is 10 LF; around 4 x 4 ft it is 16 LF (derived). The number is large for the frame alone, so the AI must read what the legend line is measuring (frame perimeter, fabric length with entrenchment, or a surrounding trap) before accepting it.

### 3.3 Construction exit and stone
- Area = the drawn pad. Georgia minimum 20 ft x 50 ft = 1,000 SF; the job's pad is 1,569.4 SF (derived from the legend vs minimum) (GSWCC Co p.74, primary; project C-2.0).
- Stone volume = pad SF x thickness / 27. For 6 in: 1,569.4 x 0.5 / 27 = 29.06 CY (derived). Geotextile = pad area (the full length and width) (GSWCC Co p.74, primary), 1,569.4 SF = 174.4 SY (derived).
- Stone is bought in tons: 1.35-1.5 tons per CY loose for clean crushed stone (model). For 29.06 CY, that is 39-44 tons (derived). Confirm with the quarry.
- The estimate line for the exit bundles geotextile, wash rack and "culvert under entrance if required" as one SF line (project line 684): treat the wash rack and culvert as conditional items to flag, not quantities.
- NC adds that entrances should be at least 12 ft per lane of traffic and flared where they meet pavement ([NC 6.06](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/erosion-and-sediment-control/esc-design-manual/chapter-60-site-preparation-jan-2026-revision/open), primary; [EPA track-out controls](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-construction-track-out-controls.pdf), primary). Do not add flare area unless the plan draws it.

### 3.4 Disturbed-area stabilization and slope matting
- Measure each hatch as plan SF and keep the practice code with it. The Georgia practices stack on the same ground: Ds1 + Ds2 + Du are drawn on the **same hatch** (project C-2.0 legend "DS1, DS2 & DU"). One SF area is therefore the base for three material lines (mulch, temporary seed, dust control); do not read it as three separate areas (project).
- Do not double count across overlapping hatches or phases. The C-2.0 legend measures two stabilization hatches, 20,298.9 SF and 15,581.9 SF; the estimate files the first under C-2.0 (lines 689-691) and the second under C-2.1 (lines 694-696), although the C-2.1 legend itself shows only the 2,114 SF "SS, DS3" area (project legends and lines.json). The sum 35,880.8 SF is 91.5 percent (8.5 percent under) of the 39,204 SF (0.9 acre) of total disturbance stated on C-2.3 (derived). Whether the 15,581.9 SF hatch is a subset of the 20,298.9 SF hatch or a second area is exactly the question to resolve from the hatch geometry before adding; on this job the p65 render shows two separate polygons that do not overlap (red over the two existing building outlines, teal around them).
- Rate-based materials: convert SF to acres (43,560 SF/acre) and multiply by the rate on the sheet. Rates quoted on the job's own detail (project C-2.5): dry straw 2 tons/acre, dry hay 2.5 tons/acre, wood cellulose with hydroseeding 500 lb/acre (1,000 lb/acre with tackifier on 3/4:1 or steeper), agricultural lime 1-2 tons/acre. Derived per 1,000 SF: straw 91.8 lb, hay 114.8 lb, cellulose 11.5 lb (22.9 lb with tackifier), lime 45.9-91.8 lb. For 20,298.88 SF (0.466 acre) straw at 2 tons/acre is 0.93 ton (derived). Seed and fertilizer rates by species are in the C-2.4 table (project) and the Georgia Ds2/Ds3 tables ([GSWCC](https://gaswcc.georgia.gov/document/document/field-manual-erosion-and-sediment-control-georgia-2016-edition-pdf/download) pp.20-27, primary). Straw mulch can also be specified by depth: straw or hay 2-4 in, wood waste 2-3 in (GSWCC Ds1 p.19, primary); the EPA typical rate for straw is 1-2 tons/acre, bark 5-8 tons/acre, wood fiber 0.5-1 ton/acre, hydraulic mulch 1.25-2.5 tons/acre ([EPA mulching](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-mulching.pdf), primary).
- Mulch is not required over erosion control blankets or block sod (project C-2.5: "when using temporary erosion control blankets or block sod, mulch is not required"). So an area covered by blanket or sod should not also carry a mulch line.
- Slope SF is surface area, not plan area, for blankets and matting: surface factor = sqrt(1 + (V/H)^2) = 1.031 for 4:1, 1.054 for 3:1, 1.118 for 2:1, 1.414 for 1:1 (derived). The job's "SS, DS3" area of 2,114 SF is a plan polygon measured in the markup (project C-2.1 legend); on 2:1 embankments (project C-2.3 states embankments are 2:1 or less) the true blanket surface is about 12 percent larger (derived), unless the estimator deliberately measures plan area.
- RECP installation rules that set the laps and anchors, [NC 6.17](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/erosion-and-sediment-control/esc-design-manual/chapter-61-surface-stabilization-jan-2026-revision/open) (primary): terminal anchor trench 12 in deep x 6 in wide at the top (rolls start 2-3 ft over the top of the slope), side overlap at least 3 in, staples or pins on a 3 ft center-to-center pattern, check slots (6 in x 6 in) every 25 ft in channels, end splices overlapped 1 ft shingled downslope and anchored every 1 ft, staples 11 gauge and at least 6 in long. NC's older netting practice: top end buried in a trench at least 6 in deep, staples every 12 in at the top, every 3 ft at edges and center, 3 in side overlap, 18 in end overlap ([NC 6.14](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/erosion-and-sediment-control/esc-design-manual/chapter-61-surface-stabilization-jan-2026-revision/open), primary). The Georgia job detail (C-2.5 "Typical installation guidelines for RECP") shows the same check-slot and overlap scheme (project).
- A 3 ft grid is one staple per 9 SF = 1 staple per SY before overlaps and trenches (derived). Slope-based staples per SY above that (about 1.2-2.0 at 3:1 to 2:1) come from the model only and from manufacturer pattern guides I could not read ([model]).
- Roll sizes: 100 SY (8 ft x 112.5 ft) and 80 SY (6 ft 8 in x 108 ft) are the usual blanket rolls (model; check: 8 x 112.5 / 9 = 100 SY, derived).
- Du (dust control) is an area, normally the same SF as the disturbed area it protects, and is met with mulch, vegetation or adhesives (GSWCC Du pp.42-43, primary).

### 3.5 Landscaping: plants, mulch, sod, topsoil
- **Plants:** quantity from the plant schedule, cross-checked against the "(n) NAME" callouts on the plan. When they disagree, carry the schedule, raise an RFI, and show both counts. On this job the schedule says American holly 5 and eastern redcedar 11, but the plan text reads "(11) American holly", "(1) eastern redcedar", "(5) eastern redcedar", which sums to holly 11 and redcedar 6 (17 trees against the schedule's 16); anise callouts (7 + 7 + 3 = 17) and wax myrtle (6 + 9 = 15) agree with the schedule (project L-1.0 text layer; callout placement not individually verified). The plan itself states "all quantities are for a general guide only; contractor shall confirm all quantities prior to bidding" (project L-1.0), and notes 8 and 20 of L-1.1 repeat that the contractor verifies quantities.
- **Count from spacing** (only when no count is given): plants = area / (spacing^2) for a square layout and area / (0.866 x spacing^2) for equilateral-triangle layout, which is 1.1547x the square count (derived; same in model). 1,000 SF at 3 ft: 111 square, 128 triangular (derived). Round up and treat as a low-confidence estimate.
- **Mulch by volume:** 1 cubic yard covers 324 SF at 1 in deep, so CY = SF x depth(in) / 324 ([Clemson HGIC](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/mulch/), secondary). That is 3.09 CY per 1,000 SF per inch (derived). At the job's 3 in depth, 2,500 SF is 23.1 CY net (derived). Clemson's recommended depths: fine wood/bark 2-3 in, coarse bark nuggets up to 4 in, pine needles and chips 2-3 in for weed control ([Clemson HGIC](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/mulch/), secondary).
- **Pine straw by the bale:** bales = ceil(bed SF / verified SF per bale). Coverage per bale varies by bale size and settled depth; the model's budgeting allowance is 25-40 SF per bale at a settled 3 in, with 40-60 SF per bale as the advertised range (model, low confidence). Derived for 2,500 SF: 84 bales at 30 SF, 63 at 40, 50 at 50, 42 at 60. The estimate line carries pine straw as 2,500 SF (project line 705); the bale count is a procurement conversion and must state the coverage assumed.
- **Sod:** measure net hatched sod area from the landscape or ESPC plan and subtract planting beds, walks and pads inside the hatch. Georgia requires fine grading to remove stones and clods (GSWCC Ds4 p.38: larger than 1 in; the job's L-1.1 note 21 and 24: remove rocks and debris, rake smooth, roll). NC: strips perpendicular to the slope, staggered brick pattern, no overlap or stretching, pegs or staples on slopes of 3:1 or steeper, then roll ([NC 6.12](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/erosion-and-sediment-control/esc-design-manual/chapter-61-surface-stabilization-jan-2026-revision/open), primary; [EPA sodding](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-sodding.pdf), primary). Pegs/staples therefore follow from slope. Sod is sold by the pallet; the model says slab and small-roll pallets cover about 400-500 SF, big-roll pallets 500-700 SF, and gives a waste allowance of 2-5 percent on rectangular areas, 5-10 percent on irregular areas and 10-15 percent on fragmented work (model; supplier-specific, unverified). Derived for 14,393.68 SF at 450 SF/pallet: 32.0 pallets net, 33.6 with 5 percent, 35.2 with 10 percent.
- **Sod appears twice on this job:** L-1.0 plant list gives sod 11,500 SF (project L-1.0 legend), and C-2.2 Ds4 gives 14,393.7 SF of sodding (project C-2.2 legend); the estimate carries both (lines 700 and 706) (project). They may be different scopes (landscape finish vs. ESPC stabilization) or the same ground measured twice. Resolve against the hatch geometry; do not add them blindly.
- **Topsoil:** NC requires at least 2 in of suitable topsoil, spread to a minimum compacted depth of 2 in on 3:1 slopes and 4 in on flatter ground, with stripping depth usually 4-6 in ([NC 6.04](https://www.deq.nc.gov/energy-mineral-and-land-resources/erosion-and-sediment-control/esc-design-manual/chapter-60-site-preparation-jan-2026-revision/open), primary). Volume = SF x depth(in) / 12 / 27; one acre-inch is 134.4 CY (derived). Georgia's notes say topsoil is used for parking-lot islands when sufficient is available (project L-1.1 note 21); the job's quantity must say whether topsoil is on-site stockpile or imported.
- **Tree pits and rings:** root-ball pit width 3 to 5 times the root-ball width, 3 in mulch ring kept off the stems, top of root ball 2-3 in above finished grade, stakes 24 in x 2 in x 2 in at 120 degrees with straps (project L-1.1 detail). Tree stakes are counted only for trees the notes say to stake ("stake trees if environmental conditions warrant", project L-1.1 note 5). So tree staking is conditional and the AI should flag it.
- **Irrigation:** if no irrigation plan is in the set, carry it as a design-build lump sum and list the scope from the notes (mains and laterals Class 200 PVC, sleeves Sch 40, 100 percent coverage, zones for lawn, shrubs/trees and flowers), plus the backflow device counted on the utility sheet (project L-1.1 notes 16-20, 25; estimate line 739 "2: irrigation backflow", 2 EA, from C-4.0/C-5.0).

### 3.6 Stormwater pond / infiltration basin
- **Plan area:** the pond polygon on C-3.0 (3,074 SF) (project). The estimator's stone storage quantity is exactly pond area x 5.5 ft / 27 = 626.185 CY, where 5.5 ft = 435.0 (bottom of basin) minus 429.5 (bottom of gravel) on C-4.2 (derived and project: line 711, 626.185 CY). So the convention was area x full gravel depth, with no void deduction and no allowance for the basin sides.
- **Order quantity vs. storage:** the stone is an order quantity (solid CY or tons), whereas storage is a volume of voids: porosity (voids / total) is commonly taken as 0.35-0.40 for clean open-graded stone (model); at 0.40 the 16,907 CF of stone holds 6,763 CF (derived). Do not apply the porosity to the stone order; use it only to check the plan's stated storage (the C-4.2 detail states WQv 2,225 CF) (project).
- **Tons:** 1.35-1.5 tons per CY for #57/#3 stone (model); the estimate line stays in CY. 626.185 CY is 845-939 tons (derived).

Typical seam allowances of 12-24 in (up to 36 in or sewn on weak subgrades) are (model) and AASHTO M 288 governs the product class. So the plan-area quantity is a floor, not a full takeoff: read the detail for wrap and top cover.
- **Underdrain:** 6 in perforated HDPE, 96 LF (project line 710); count the cleanouts, tees, caps and the connection to the outlet structure from the detail, not from the LF.
- **Side slopes, stabilization and sod:** C-4.2 states slopes no steeper than 3:1 (project). The basin's grassed slopes belong in the stabilization / sod area (and a Georgia Ds4 sod on slopes of 3:1 or steeper needs pegs or staples, GSWCC Ds4 p.39, primary); L-1.0 also directs the contractor to refer to the erosion control plans for vegetative cover on pond slopes and bottom (project L-1.0).

The ESPC retrofit at the outlet (Rt-B 5.1 FT, Rt-P 7.5 FT in the legends) is temporary and separate from the permanent stone bed (project C-2.0, C-2.1 legends).
- Pond earthwork is carried with grading, not here ("pond earthwork has been added to the grading quantity", project line 708); do not add excavation twice.

## 6. Special cases

1. **Phases.** ESPC plans are phased (here 1 to 3). The same practice code can appear on several phases and quantities may be a subset, a carry-over, or a new area. Mark every quantity with phase and code; do not subtotal practice codes across phases without checking the geometry (project C-2.0 to C-2.2).
2. **Multi-practice hatches.** One hatch carries several codes ("DS1, DS2 & DU"). It is one area feeding several lines (project C-2.0).
3. **Pre-printed manual pages on detail sheets.** The detail sheets are scans of the Georgia manual (project C-2.5, C-2.6). They contain general text (for example "Ds1 ... apply at the appropriate depth, refer to Table 1") that is not a project requirement for every option; only the practices called out in the legend count.
4. **Items by others / conditional.** "Culvert under entrance if required," "tire washer," "approved prefabricated container," "tree staking if conditions warrant," and the irrigation controller power are all conditional or by others; list them as flagged allowances with no invented quantity (project line 684; C-2.5; L-1.1).
5. **No hatch on the plan.** If a stabilization area is stated on the plan only as a number, carry the number and cite the sheet; if it is only a hatch, measure it at the plan scale and say so.
6. **Alternate BMPs.** The Georgia equivalent BMP list allows substitutions (e.g. a different sock for a type of fence) ([GSWCC EBMPL](https://gaswcc.georgia.gov/document/document/equivalent-bmp-list-2021-update-07232026/download), referenced on the GSWCC site, link not read). Quantities follow the BMP drawn, not the alternative.

## 7. Common mistakes and how to check a takeoff

| Mistake | Check |
|---|---|
| Counting stabilization areas twice (overlapping hatches; carry-over between phases) | Sum of distinct stabilization areas should not exceed the stated total disturbance (here 39,204 SF). Derived: 20,298.9 + 15,581.9 = 35,880.8 SF, 91.5 percent (8.5 percent under) of disturbance, so the two hatches are plausibly separate (the p65 render confirms they do not overlap), but a third carry-over (2,114 SF) must be checked. |
| Treating Ds1, Ds2 and Du as three areas | One hatch, three material lines. |
| Using a default stake or post spacing in place of the detail | Compute from the detail: 2 ft vs 10 ft on 767.9 LF changes the stake count from 385 to 78 (derived). |
| Counting fence length as drawn without upturns / J-hooks / sock sleeves | Compare drawn LF to the sum of segments; add physical length consumed by end upturns and sleeves only if not drawn (EPA; NC 6.66). |
| Ordering rolls by total LF rather than by run | Rolls are consumed per continuous run (model formula: R = max(1, ceil((L - o)/(100 - o)))). |
| Counting blanket by plan area on steep slopes | Use slope surface factor (1.118 at 2:1) and add trench/lap material (NC 6.17; derived). |
| Mulching areas covered by blankets or sod | The project note excludes mulch under blankets and block sod (project C-2.5). |
| Plant counts from the plan but not the schedule (or the reverse) | Compare both; flag differences. Here: holly 5 vs 11, redcedar 11 vs 6 (project L-1.0). |
| Mixing mulch units: SF, CY, bales | State depth and coverage per unit; 324 SF per CY per inch (Clemson, secondary); bales need a verified SF per bale. |
| Applying stone porosity to the order | Storage uses porosity; the order is solid volume or tons (model). |
| Geotextile only as plan area | A wrapped bed needs sides, top closure and seams; state what the quantity covers (section 3.6). |
| Double-counting pond earthwork | The estimate says excavation is in the grading quantity (project line 708). |
| Sod in two places (landscape and ESPC) | Reconcile 11,500 SF with 14,393.7 SF (project). |
| Reading sheet legend labels loosely | Legend names (for example "TRMPORARY INLETE SEDIMENT TRAP", "ST-1", "SS, DS3: AREA") are noisy; match the practice code to the legend table on the phase sheet (project legends). |
| Counting trees under demolition and landscape | C-1.0 key note 8 "remove existing trees/vegetation, 2 EA" is demolition (project line 10). |

Cross-check ratios that are worth running on any job: LF of sediment barrier per 10,000 SF disturbed (EPA's floor is 100); acres disturbed vs. the area on the plan; total of phase-by-phase stabilization vs. the disturbance stated on the notes; plant counts vs. the schedule; pond stone CY vs. plan area x depth (here exactly 3,074 x 5.5 / 27 = 626.185) (derived).

Gaps between this job's estimate and the legends that the AI should flag, not silently fix (project, quantities only): line 699 carries 194 LF of inlet protection on C-2.2 while the C-2.2 legend measures 74.5 FT of curb inlet protection (the 194 matches the C-2.1 legend, 193.7 FT); and the 15,581.9 SF stabilization hatch is measured on the C-2.0 legend but filed under C-2.1 in the estimate (lines 694-696).

## 8. What an AI takeoff must read from the drawings and specs

From the ESPC phase plans: phase number, every legend code with its symbol, each hatched area and its code stack, each linear device with its code and extent, count of inlets by protection type, the construction exit pad polygon, the washout and fuel-storage symbols, the retrofit extents, and the limits of disturbance and total area (0.9 acre) from the notes (project C-2.0 to C-2.3).

From the ESPC details and notes: fabric type and height (A/B/C), post or stake spacing and depth, sock diameter, stone size and thickness, geotextile, lime/fertilizer/seed/mulch rates, anchor and staple patterns, and which options are pasted manual text rather than selected practices (project C-2.5, C-2.6; GSWCC).

From the grading and detail sheets: pond area, bottom elevations, stone depth, slopes (3:1 maximum), liner and geotextile layers, underdrain size and length, outlet structure and trash rack, no-compaction rule (project C-3.0, C-4.0, C-4.2).

From the landscape plan and details: plant schedule rows (common and botanical name, size, root condition, quantity, spacing), plan callouts, hatch legend (sod/grass, mulch), mulch depth, planting pit geometry, staking rule, tree protection, irrigation notes, and the line "all quantities are for a general guide only" (project L-1.0, L-1.1).

From the specifications: confirm that no Division 31/32 section exists (project spec index) and carry the drawing notes as the specification; look in Division 01 only for temporary controls, waste and allowance language (project spec index).

Method rules for the AI: use the legend row name verbatim and keep unit as shown; quote a source sheet for each quantity; carry both numbers when a schedule and a plan disagree; use integer-friendly derivation with every constant named; compute stakes, posts, pallets, bales and tons from stated spacing and coverage rather than from defaults; flag every conditional ("if required", "by others", "no details so assumed"). Compare with the app's engine, which already has an assembly for each family (sediment barrier, inlet protection, construction exit, check dam, washout, stabilization, outlet protection, linear practice, tree protection, plants, mulch, sod, seed, topsoil, edging, irrigation); its defaults are stake spacing 10 ft for sock (this job's detail says 2 ft), 6 ft for silt fence posts, 100 LF fabric rolls, 1.4 tons/CY stone, 50 SF per pine-straw bale, 15 percent topsoil settlement (project `lib/quantities/trades/site/erosion-control.ts`, `landscaping.ts`). The engine already has a pond/basin assembly (`site-utilities.basin`: reference area, storage stone, geotextile with sides and optional top wrap, underdrain), and `app-gaps.md` runs it on this job. The gaps that remain: no separate `stoneBedAreas` input on the basin (one `areas` input feeds both the pond reference line and the stone and fabric), no measured `runs` input on `erosion-control.inlet-protection` (it makes LF only from a grate size or curb opening), geotextile and blanket laps added in net mode, and no slope-surface factor for blankets.
