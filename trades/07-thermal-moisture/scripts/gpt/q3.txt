## 1. Basis: convention versus named standard

- **[C] Estimating convention:** The LF/SF/piece-count practices below are common takeoff methods, **not a universal measurement standard**. State your assumptions and follow any measurement rules in the contract.
- **[G] Geometry/unit conversion:** The area, overlap, and sealant-yield formulas below are mathematical relationships.
- **[S] Named references:**
  - **SMACNA, *Architectural Sheet Metal Manual*** provides sheet-metal detailing, fabrication, and installation guidance.
  - **ASTM C1193**, *Standard Guide for Use of Joint Sealants*, addresses sealant-joint design and installation.
  - **ASTM C920** specifies elastomeric sealant performance/classification.
  - **ASTM C1330** specifies cylindrical sealant backing.

These references **do not establish one universal 10-ft/12-ft stock length, lap allowance, or estimating waste percentage**. Use the specified edition, project details, and approved manufacturer/fabricator requirements.

## 2. What quantities to list **[C]**

For metal, maintain three related quantities:

1. **Net installed LF:** Length of the completed flashing or coping.
2. **Developed—or “girth”—SF:** Sheet area needed to form the profile.
3. **EA/pieces:** Straight sections, corners, end caps, splice plates, and other fittings.

These are different descriptions of the same work—not three additive material quantities.

Separate items by material, thickness/gauge, finish, profile/girth, and detail.

| Item | Main installed quantity | Developed-area quantity | Piece/accessory quantities |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coping** | LF along each parapet run, using a declared reference line | All profile legs: top, front/rear drops, returns, hems, etc. | Straight sections by length; inside/outside corners; intersections; end caps; splice/cover plates; expansion-joint assemblies |
| **Through-wall flashing** | LF at each distinct flashing elevation/run | Full developed path through/behind the wall, including upstands, cavity crossing, outward projection, drip, hems, and returns | Straight sections; corner assemblies; end dams; transitions; splice components |
| **Counterflashing** | LF along each roof-to-wall or similar termination | Exposed leg plus reglet insertion, offsets, kicks, hems, and returns | Straight sections; corners; terminations; splice components; separate receivers if required |
| **Head flashing** | LF for each opening: required head length, including specified end extensions | Back/upstand leg, horizontal or sloped cover, face/drop, drip, hem, and returns | EA heads by opening size; end dams/returns; corner and splice components where applicable |
| **Sills** | LF by opening, plus EA sill assemblies | Actual sloped sill depth, rear upstand, front drop/drip, hems, and returns | EA sills by size; end dams/end caps; corner and splice components |
| **Joint sealant** | LF of **each individual sealant bead**, grouped by product and joint width × depth | Convert bead volume into packages | Cartridges/sausages by actual package volume |
| **Backer rod** | LF of each joint receiving rod, grouped by diameter/type | Normally not taken off in SF | Rolls or packages after applying the stated cutting/waste allowance |

For head flashings and sills, an opening schedule is useful: **opening ID, quantity, finished length, profile, and end treatment**.

**Ancillary metal:** If included in scope, list continuous cleats/receivers in LF and their own girth SF; intermittent clips and fasteners in EA. Do not assume their lengths or spacings equal those of the face metal.

## 3. How to measure girth SF **[G, with C takeoff basis]**

**Girth is the developed flat-sheet width—not merely the exposed face or coping top width.**

For a constant profile:

\[
A_{\text{SF}}=\frac{L_{\text{ft}}\times G_{\text{in}}}{12}
\]

Example:

\[
100\text{ LF}\times24\text{-in girth}/12=200\text{ SF}
\]

For fabrication quantities, use the required blank lengths:

\[
A_{\text{blank}}=
\sum\frac{L_{\text{blank, ft}}\times G_{\text{blank, in}}}{12}
+\text{separately calculated special-fitting blank areas}
\]

### Include in the developed width
- Front and rear legs.
- Concealed upstands and insertion legs.
- Drips, kicks, offsets, and return legs.
- Material consumed by hems.
- Actual sloped dimensions, rather than horizontal projections alone.

The final blank width should reflect the fabricator’s bend allowances/deductions. Simply adding outside profile dimensions is an estimating approximation.

### Keep these distinct
- **Installed LF:** System coverage.
- **Blank SF:** Actual metal, including required overlaps and fitting allowances.
- **Stock sheets/lengths:** Procurement quantities after nesting, cuts, and usable-offcut decisions.

An end dam formed from an integral tab needs metal beyond the basic constant-section strip. Calculate that tab/blank allowance; do not also count it as a separate fabricated part unless it actually is separate.

## 4. Corners, miters, end caps, and splice plates **[C]**

### Corners and miters
- Count each physical change of direction as **one corner assembly**, classified by inside/outside, angle, and profile.
- A corner assembled from two mitered straight pieces is still **one corner location**.
- If shop operations are tracked, list **two miter cuts** where applicable—but do not turn those into two corner assemblies.
- Count nonstandard corners, tees, and intersections separately.

For a prefabricated corner with two legs:

- Include its coverage in the overall installed LF.
- **Deduct that coverage from adjoining straight-section quantities**.
- Count the corner itself in EA and calculate its own blank area if required.

Do not count full straight-run metal through a corner and then add a complete corner fitting over the same coverage.

Declare whether plan lengths follow a centerline, inside face, or outside face. **Miter cutting lengths require the actual long-point/short-point geometry**, not just the takeoff reference length.

### End caps and end dams
- Count **one end cap per actual open end requiring a cap**.
- Count **one end dam per required flashing termination**.
- A typical isolated sill or head may require two end dams, but that is a detail-dependent assumption—not a universal rule.
- Distinguish integral, shop-welded, and separate loose components.

A wall intersection is not automatically an end cap; it may require a return, transition, or sealant termination instead.

### Splice/cover plates
Count **actual connections requiring a plate**, not “one plate per piece.”

- One open chain of \(n\) connected sections ordinarily has \(n-1\) internal connections.
- Connections to prefabricated corners may need additional plates.
- A closed loop of \(n\) total connected sections has \(n\) connections.
- Integral welded corners do not create field splice-plate quantities merely because they contain a bend.

Classify ordinary splice plates, backing plates, and expansion-joint components separately. If one joint requires multiple components, list each component.

## 5. Piece lengths and lap allowances **[C + G]**

### 10-ft versus 12-ft sections
Neither is a universal standard. Record:

- Actual fabricated length.
- Whether the quoted length means **overall metal length** or **net installed coverage**.
- Required lap or joint gap.
- Maximum permitted section length.
- Corner-leg lengths and terminal allowances.

Fabrication equipment, available sheet sizes, handling, finish, and thermal-movement details can affect allowable lengths.

### Overlapping straight pieces

For one uninterrupted run with:
- \(R\) = required coverage, ft
- \(S\) = actual overall piece length, ft
- \(a\) = overlap at each joint, ft
- \(n\) = number of pieces

and exactly \(n-1\) equal overlaps:

\[
R_{\text{covered}}=nS-(n-1)a
\]

Thus, for \(R>0\):

\[
n=\max\left(1,\left\lceil\frac{R-a}{S-a}\right\rceil\right)
\]

Required total cut-piece length is:

\[
L_{\text{blank,total}}=R+(n-1)a
\]

This simplified formula excludes special fittings and additional terminal overlaps; handle those separately.

**Illustration only—not a standard lap:** With a 40-ft run and a 2-in lap:

| Actual piece length | Pieces required | Total cut-piece length | Full stock length before offcut reuse |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 ft | 5 | 40 ft 8 in | 50 ft |
| 12 ft | 4 | 40 ft 6 in | 48 ft |

Four actual 10-ft pieces cover only **39 ft 6 in** after three 2-in overlaps.

### Important lap rules
- Use the lap specified for that particular material and detail.
- Calculate laps from the **actual joint count**.
- A lap along the run increases blank length; it does not automatically increase girth.
- Butt joints with splice plates are **not overlapping joints**. Account for metal lengths, specified gaps, and plates separately.
- Corner connections and terminal overlaps must be included where required.
- Do not add a lap percentage after laps are already included explicitly.
- Keep cutting waste separate from intentional laps.
- Develop a run-by-run cut list; then optimize reusable offcuts. Dividing the building’s total LF by 10 or 12 can miss short-run and fitting requirements.

## 6. Sealant and backer-rod quantities

### Sealant LF **[C]**

Measure the length of **every required bead**, separated by width × depth and sealant type.

Examples:

- One continuous bead along a 100-ft run = **100 LF**.
- Two specified continuous beads along that run = **200 LF**.
- Interior and exterior perimeter seals are **separate beads**; count both when specified.
- Sealant at transverse flashing joints is measured along each required seam path—not automatically along the entire flashing run.
- Two beads under a lap count as two bead lengths.

For a coping splice, the specified sealant path might cross the top only, follow more of the developed profile, or consist of concealed beads. Use the actual detail.

### Cartridge/sausage yield **[G]**

For a rectangular-equivalent joint:

- \(w\) = joint width, inches
- \(d\) = sealant depth, inches
- \(L\) = bead length, feet

\[
V_{\text{required, in}^3}=12Lwd
\]

Theoretical package yield:

\[
\boxed{Y_{\text{LF/package}}=
\frac{V_{\text{package, in}^3}}{12wd}}
\]

Since:

\[
1\text{ US fluid oz}=1.8046875\text{ in}^3
\]

then:

\[
\boxed{Y_{\text{LF/package}}=
\frac{0.150390625\times V_{\text{package, US fl oz}}}{wd}}
\]

For packages rated in mL:

\[
Y_{\text{LF/package}}=
\frac{V_{\text{package, mL}}}{196.645\,wd}
\]

**Use volume, not ounces of weight.** A 600-mL sausage and a 20-US-fl-oz sausage are close, but not identical in volume.

#### Theoretical yield, before losses

| Joint width × depth | 10.1-US-fl-oz cartridge | 20-US-fl-oz sausage |
|---|---:|---:|
| ¼ in × ¼ in | 24.3 LF | 48.1 LF |
| ⅜ in × ¼ in | 16.2 LF | 32.1 LF |
| ½ in × ¼ in | 12.2 LF | 24.1 LF |

If \(u\) is the usable fraction of each package:

\[
Y_{\text{usable}}=uY_{\text{theoretical}}
\]

\[
N_{\text{packages}}=
\left\lceil
\frac{12\sum(L_iw_id_i)}
{uV_{\text{package, in}^3}}
\right\rceil
\]

Group quantities by compatible product, color, and package before rounding. State the loss assumption; avoid applying both a usable-fraction deduction and another allowance for the same loss.

**Depth means sealant depth, not total joint-cavity depth.** If the bead is triangular, concave, or otherwise nonrectangular, use its actual cross-sectional area or the manufacturer’s yield data. Do not assume every joint has the same width-to-depth ratio.

### Backer rod **[C; selection governed by detail/manufacturer]**
- Measure LF only where rod is required.
- Separate by diameter and material/type.
- Include cut/end waste explicitly.
- Select diameter and compression according to the specified system and manufacturer.
- Do not automatically assign backer rod to concealed lap-sealant beads or joints requiring bond-breaker tape instead.
- If both faces require separate backing and sealant, count both backing runs.

## 7. Common takeoff mistakes **[C]**

- **Double-counting corner coverage:** Full straight lengths plus complete corner legs.
- **Missing concealed metal:** Rear legs, reglet insertions, hems, returns, end-dam tabs, or sloped development.
- **Confusing top width with girth:** Coping top SF is not total sheet-metal SF.
- **Treating every segment endpoint as an end cap:** Many endpoints are splices or transitions.
- **Counting one splice plate per piece:** Connections—not pieces—control the count.
- **Assuming nominal stock length equals coverage:** Overlaps reduce coverage unless already built into the stated nominal length.
- **Adding laps twice:** Once in piece lengths and again as a blanket allowance.
- **Missing the second sealant face:** Or doubling all joints even where only one face is specified.
- **Using joint width × full cavity depth:** This can substantially overstate sealant volume.
- **Omitting transverse or concealed beads:** Continuous perimeter sealant may not be the entire scope.
- **Counting integral parts twice:** Integral end dams, caps, or corner fabrication also listed as separate material.

## 8. Final self-check

- [ ] Every plan/elevation run and flashing level is accounted for.
- [ ] LF reference lines and opening-length conventions are stated.
- [ ] Profiles include all visible and concealed legs.
- [ ] Net LF, blank SF, stock quantities, and EA fittings reconcile.
- [ ] Corner coverage is credited against straight sections.
- [ ] Corners, end caps, end dams, transitions, and expansion joints are separately identified.
- [ ] Piece counts use actual fabricated lengths and the correct joint type.
- [ ] Laps, terminal allowances, gaps, and waste are each included once.
- [ ] Splice counts match the connection layout, including corner connections.
- [ ] Sealant LF includes every required bead and face.
- [ ] Sealant widths/depths and package volumes are verified.
- [ ] Backer-rod type, diameter, and LF match the applicable joints.
- [ ] Missing dimensions or unconfirmed assumptions are clearly flagged.
